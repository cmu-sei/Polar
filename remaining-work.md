## Found by the load harness, 2026-10-21 (docs/architecture/broker-baseline.md)

Fixed, with tests: the broker shutting down on a subscription it could not make (`2bc47be7`), topics keeping ended
subscribers (`dc06e841`), unanswered subscriptions holding up publishing (`631fb0e1`), ractor output ports dropping a
subscriber that falls behind (`92bc4822`), every publish formatting its payload into a span (`dfb432cb`), and
`polar-init` waiting in init for good (`8030abdd`). Open:

- **Pods of one workload share an identity**, and the broker keeps one live session per identity (a new one supersedes
  the rest and closes their connections). So a workload cannot run several pods at once, and the harness cannot measure
  several connections. Horizontal scaling is wanted (David, 2026-10-22), and needs two things, not one
  (`docs/architecture/cassini-broker.md`, "One session per identity, and fan-out"):
  - *Who a connection is:* a replica runs beside the others, a replacement takes over; the broker cannot tell them apart
    from the certificate today. Options: an identity per pod (a workload per pod, or a pod-instance name in the
    certificate), or sessions keyed by pod instance as well as identity.
  - *Who gets a message:* topics fan out to every subscriber, so replicas with identities of their own would each
    process every message. Scaling a consumer or processor needs consumer groups: replicas share a subscription and each
    message goes to one of them; ungrouped subscribers keep fan-out. The workload is the natural group, the pod its
    member; the supersede rule would then apply per member. Observers that scale out must split their source instead.
  - Cedar decides per workload either way (the replicas hold the same authority), so stage 5b and its cache do not
    depend on this. Not needed yet; decide before any workload runs more than one replica.
- The `cassini-client` CLI's `publish` does not take a broker refusal as its answer: it logs the refusal, then waits out
  its 10 s acknowledgement timeout and fails with "Timed out waiting for publish ack" (seen 2026-10-22 probing the
  sandbox's broker). It should fail at once with the refusal's reason.
- The Cassini client's read loop goes on after a failed `read_exact`, reading from the middle of a frame (`cassini/client/src/lib.rs`,
  the read loop); it should end the transport.
- Stage 5b is measured against the baseline, without and with its decision cache: done 2026-10-22
  (`docs/architecture/broker-baseline.md`, "Stage 5b: the broker deciding").
- **Security: three of the credentials agent's topics append whatever they carry to its ledger** (found 2026-10-22;
  `docs/architecture/authority-model.md`, "The consumer's request topics, audited"). `credentials-grants`,
  `credentials-revocations` and `credentials-account-provisioning` take a raw `CredentialsEvent`, any kind, with no
  signature and no publisher, and append it. Whoever can publish on one can provision an account holding
  `c:update-policy` and claim it, grant `p:` names, or revoke an administrator. Only the broker's floor stops it, since
  stage 5b (nothing declares publishing on them); a Cedar `permit` would open it, and before stage 5b nothing stopped
  it. The signed administrator actions already carry all three, so the raw topics are a parallel path nothing uses.
  **Removed 2026-10-22**: the signed administrator actions are the only way to provision, grant and revoke.
- **The token's subject is still a key in two tables after the authority model** (found 2026-10-22;
  `docs/architecture/authority-model.md`, "Decisions": one identity). cert-issuer's floor
  (`cert-issuer/server/src/floor.rs:115`) and the credentials agent's grants for workloads
  (`credentials-agent/consume/src/issuance.rs:219`, "Two names for one credential") are keyed by
  `system:serviceaccount:<namespace>:<serviceaccount>`, where everything else is keyed by Polar's one identity, which is
  in hand at both (cert-issuer's `normalized_identity`). Key both by the identity, and convert the token's subject only
  where a token is read. The issuance floor's replacement is keyed this way: since slice 8 issuance asks the authority
  model by the identity (2026-10-22).
- **The authority model** (`docs/architecture/authority-model.md`, proposed 2026-10-22, to cover with David after stage
  5b's slices 6 and 7): one model of the deployment as data, rules over it, and acceptance of a policy or groups
  document as a comparison over the closed catalog. It replaces slice 5's hand-written probes. Until it is built, a
  `forbid` on an authority path can lock a deployment out. **Being built** ("Build order, tests first"): slices 1 to 8 done by
  2026-10-22. Since slice 7 the credentials agent judges every `policy` and `groups` document before accepting it,
  so such a `forbid` is refused with its reason (open point B); since slice 8 a certificate carries what the model
  gives its workload, and the issuance floor is gone. Slices 9 (Cedar on the thirteen request topics) and 10 (deploy
  and verify) remain.
- **A person had two names in force** (found 2026-10-22 building slice 7; fixed the same day). Topic requests knew a
  person by account ID, signed documents and commands by username, and a groups entry keyed by username never applied
  to their topic requests. Settled by the 2026-10-12 decision (`credentials-policy.md`): authors write usernames, and
  the credentials agent converts them to account IDs before judging and publishing; a signer's principal is the account
  ID (`authority-model.md`, "A person is written by username and known by account ID").
- The dev host's storage: about 750 GiB of the `nix` volume group is unallocated, `/` is a 199 GB f2fs on a 400 GB
  volume (never grown), and the nix-usernetes node's containerd and kubelet state share `/home` with everything else,
  so a large Cargo `target` directory tainted the node with disk pressure and evicted every pod (2026-10-21). f2fs grows
  only unmounted. A volume of its own for the node's state would stop builds evicting the cluster.

### 1. What "migrated" means now

The old `AgentBuilder::new(..).with_health_check(..)` shape is gone (older commit messages describe it). Today (`polar-agent-builder/src/agent.rs`, `examples/hello_agent.rs`):

- The agent is a type implementing `PolarAgent`: `NAME`, `PURPOSE`, `NEEDS` (capabilities beyond the
  Cassini client), `SHUTDOWN_BUDGET`, `SECTIONS` (its own `polar-env` variables), `HELP`,
  `SELF_TEST`, `CASSINI_CLIENT`, and `async fn start(Configured)`.
- `main` is `polar_agent_builder::run::<A>()`. `run` answers `--help`, `--scaffold`, `--check --config
  FILE`, judges the configuration, and starts the agent; it never exits 0 for a started agent.
- Capabilities (`.with_graph_write()`, `.with_encryption()`, `.requires(..)`) replace
  `with_health_check`; health args come from capabilities and `POLAR_HEALTH_*`. The agent reads its own
  variables through `polar-env` declarations, not `std::env` (a `disallowed_methods` lint and a
  `clippy.toml` per crate enforce it).
- The infrastructure supervisor owns health, router, Cassini facade (reconnects with backoff and
  buffers offline publishes) and optionally crypto. `Respawned` / `InfrastructureRespawned` no
  longer exist. The domain supervisor keeps its half of `PrepareShutdown` -> drain ->
  `ProceedWithShutdown`, declares its own `SHUTDOWN_BUDGET`, and must treat the supervisor stopping as
  failure (exit non-zero so the Job restarts).
- A `Registered` arm must do health and nothing else, or guard what it spawns (`is_none()`), because
  `Registered` now fires on every registration, including a resume after a transient drop
  (see the FINDING item under "Gaps found 2026-10-08").
- Tests: `polar_agent_tests!(MyAgent)` in the agent's `agent.rs`.

- [ ] **GAP, MUST BE ADDRESSED (2026-10-14): nothing checks that a certificate presented as an access credential carries the permission for what it is used to do.** *(2026-10-21: the reading this entry asks for is done; the model, the enforcement map and the numbered gaps are in `docs/architecture/policy-enforcement.md`, the broker in `cassini-broker.md`, the topics in `topics.md`.)* The design says what a certificate's holder may communicate with is embedded in its SAN names (`p:` permissions, `cert-issuer/common/src/permission.rs`), resolved by `PermissionsActor` from the presented certificate plus supplemental permissions, and decided by Cedar in the nominal state (`polar-permissions`, `polar-policy`); the certificate is the floor and Cedar is the runtime nominal state. What is built: the credentials agent issues certificates with `p:` names from grants, runs Cedar over a requester's principal for secrets, and the builder admits signed documents by publisher. ## Environment contract (`polar-env`), decided 2026-10-07

Design note: `docs/architecture/configuration.md` (what is built, the layers, who holds the configuration, the ConfigurationActor, the credentials agent, the schema export).

Principle: declare each environment variable once, with its type, default, documentation and whether it is secret. Reading, validating and describing it all derive from that one declaration, so the list of variables cannot exist apart from the code that reads it. A component groups its variables into a section (with any relations between them); the agent builder maps each capability to the sections it brings; one `check_environment` serves `build()`, the `polar-config-check` binary that `resolve.nu check` runs, and later each agent's own check mode. Whoever owns a fact owns the rule about it: the code owns the health relations, so they live in Rust and the render calls them; the deployment owns "grace period above budget", so that stays in Nushell. In progress as part of the health work (stages below); everything else here waits until the health work is done, then gets prioritised.

- [ ] SCHEDULER AGENT, reconciliation as the answer to a lossy broker (direction, 2026-10-09; the scheduler agents have been on the builder since 2026-10-10, so this is now a note for the reconcile design). The scheduler\'s reconcile loop should not only react to changes (a privileged admin changing a repo or a schedule, which happens sporadically); it should also, periodically, send EVERY agent the state it should be operating in, as the scheduler understands it from the live configuration: desired state, re-asserted on an interval, so drift is corrected and anything the broker dropped is repaired. Requirements this puts on the receivers: applying the same desired state twice is a no-op (idempotent apply), and a message carries the whole desired state for its recipient, not a delta, so a lost one costs at most one interval. The interval is a decision (how long may an agent run on stale configuration); it should be configurable and carried in the schedule configuration.
